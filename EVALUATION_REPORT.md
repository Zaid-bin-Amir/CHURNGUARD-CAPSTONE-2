## 1. Data and split

- Rows: 7,243 customers, 19 raw input features, one binary target (`Churn`).

- Cross-validation: stratified 5-fold on the training rows only.

## 2. Leakage check of engineered features

- Target column present in model inputs: False.
- Every engineered feature is computed row by row from input columns only (no target, no statistics of other rows).
- The feature step and all encoders are fitted inside each cross-validation fold, never on the hold-out.
- Univariate strength check (flag if above 0.85): no feature flagged.

| Engineered feature | Univariate AUC | Strength | Flagged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| total_services | 0.4982 | 0.5018 | no |
| charges_per_tenure | 0.8014 | 0.8014 | no |
| contract_risk | 0.7286 | 0.7286 | no |
| auto_pay | 0.3767 | 0.6233 | no |
| security_support | 0.4281 | 0.5719 | no |
| tenure_bucket | 0.2832 | 0.7168 | no |

Justification of each engineered feature:

- `tenure_bucket`: Hypothesis: churn risk is highest early in the customer lifetime and falls with loyalty. Buckets capture that non-linear shape for linear models and give retention staff readable segments.
- `total_services`: Hypothesis: customers subscribed to more services are more embedded in the product and switch less. One count summarises nine service columns.
- `charges_per_tenure`: Hypothesis: a high monthly bill relative to time with the company marks newer customers who feel they overpay. Computed as MonthlyCharges / (tenure + 1).
- `contract_risk`: Hypothesis: month-to-month contracts carry no commitment and are the easiest to leave. The flag makes this segment explicit.
- `auto_pay`: Hypothesis: automatic payment reduces forgotten or failed payments and signals commitment.
- `security_support`: Hypothesis: customers who have both online security and tech support receive more service touchpoints and tend to stay.

### Selection against the business priority

Candidates were ranked by recall-weighted F2 (beta = 2) at each model's own best threshold: Logistic Regression (0.750), Random Forest (0.748), XGBoost (default settings) (0.743). Ranked by PR-AUC: Logistic Regression (0.676), XGBoost (default settings) (0.676), Random Forest (0.673). Accuracy was deliberately not used for selection because the majority baseline already reaches 73.5% accuracy while catching no churners. A missed churner costs the business a customer, while an unnecessary retention call costs a few minutes of an agent, so recall carries more weight than precision.

The final model is XGBoost, as named by the project brief, tuned and probability-calibrated. It is kept alongside the interpretable Logistic Regression, which stays in the repository as the explainable reference model.

## 4. Hyperparameter tuning

RandomizedSearchCV, 5 sampled configurations, stratified 5-fold, scored by PR-AUC (average precision). PR-AUC was chosen because it is sensitive to the minority class and is not inflated by the many easy non-churners. `scale_pos_weight` is part of the search so the model can trade off the class imbalance itself.

Search space:

- `n_estimators`: integer 150 to 599
- `max_depth`: integer 2 to 6
- `learning_rate`: log-uniform 0.01 to 0.2
- `subsample`: uniform 0.6 to 1.0
- `colsample_bytree`: uniform 0.5 to 1.0
- `min_child_weight`: integer 1 to 9
- `gamma`: uniform 0.0 to 2.0
- `reg_lambda`: log-uniform 0.5 to 20
- `scale_pos_weight`: uniform 1.0 to 3.5 (class imbalance weight)

Chosen configuration (best cross-validated PR-AUC 0.6797; slightly optimistic because it is the maximum over the search): `colsample_bytree` = 0.9869, `gamma` = 0.4655, `learning_rate` = 0.0131, `max_depth` = 4, `min_child_weight` = 7, `n_estimators` = 393, `reg_lambda` = 4.4469, `scale_pos_weight` = 1.1161, `subsample` = 0.8430.
